Signs You Need Emergency Plumbing

  • Water actively flowing from a pipe or fixture
  • Sewage backing up into a sink, tub, or floor drain
  • Complete loss of hot water in the home
  • A toilet that won't stop overflowing
  • Visible water damage on ceilings or walls
  • A shut-off valve that won't turn

What Typically Causes a Plumbing Emergency

  • A burst or ruptured supply line, often from age, corrosion, or freezing
  • A main sewer line blockage backing water up through multiple drains
  • A failed water heater component causing leaking or total loss of hot water
  • A cracked or disconnected fitting under a sink or behind a wall
  • Tree root intrusion damaging an underground line

Our Emergency Plumbing Process

Call & Triage

We ask a few questions to understand severity and advise on immediate steps, like locating your main shut-off.

Diagnose On-Site

We identify the source of the failure, not just the visible symptom.

Stabilize & Repair

We stop active water loss first, then complete the appropriate repair.

What to Do While You Wait

If it's safe to do so, shut off the water at the main valve or the fixture's local shut-off. Move valuables away from standing water and avoid using electrical outlets near the affected area.

Burst Pipes

A burst pipe requires immediate attention to limit water damage. See Burst Pipe Repair for what the repair process involves.

Sewer Backups

When more than one drain backs up at the same time, the problem is usually in the main line rather than a single fixture. See Sewer Line Repair.

Emergency Plumbing FAQs

What counts as a plumbing emergency?+

Active flooding, burst pipes, sewer backups affecting multiple fixtures, and complete loss of hot water are typically treated as urgent.

Should I shut off my water before you arrive?+

If you can safely locate your main shut-off valve and water is actively flowing, shutting it off can limit damage.
