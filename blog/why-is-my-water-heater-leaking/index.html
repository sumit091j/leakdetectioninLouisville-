Water Heater Guide

Why Is My Water Heater Leaking?

A leaking water heater is worth addressing quickly — both to prevent water damage and because a leak can point to a larger underlying issue.

Common Causes

  • A failed pressure relief valve releasing water when pressure builds too high
  • Corrosion inside the tank itself, often related to a worn anode rod
  • Loose or worn fittings and connections
  • Condensation, which can sometimes be mistaken for a leak

How to Tell If It's Serious

Water pooling consistently at the base of the tank, rather than a one-time drip, usually points to a real leak rather than condensation. If the tank itself is corroded, replacement is often more practical than repair.

What to Do Next

If you notice standing water, shut off the water supply and power (or gas) to the unit if it's safe to do so, and have the unit inspected before the leak worsens.

FAQs

Is it safe to keep using a leaking water heater?+

It depends on the source and severity of the leak — it's best to have it inspected before continuing to use the unit.

Does a leak always mean I need a new water heater?+

Not always. Some leaks come from a replaceable valve or fitting rather than the tank itself.
