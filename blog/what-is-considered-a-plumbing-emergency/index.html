Emergency Plumbing Guide

What Is Considered a Plumbing Emergency?

Not every plumbing problem needs an immediate call. But some situations can cause serious damage the longer they go unaddressed. Here's how to tell the difference.

Situations That Typically Need Immediate Attention

  • Water actively flowing from a burst or ruptured pipe
  • Sewage backing up into more than one drain
  • Complete loss of hot water in the home
  • A toilet that continues overflowing after shutting off the supply valve
  • Visible water damage spreading across a ceiling or wall

Situations That Can Usually Wait for a Scheduled Visit

  • A single slow drain
  • A dripping faucet
  • A running toilet that isn't overflowing
  • Minor fluctuations in water pressure

What to Do in the Meantime

If you're dealing with an active leak or burst pipe, locate your home's main water shut-off valve and turn it off before calling. This limits water damage while you wait for a plumber to arrive.

FAQs

Should I turn off my water before calling a plumber?+

If water is actively leaking or flowing, shutting off the main valve can limit damage while you wait.

Is a slow drain an emergency?+

Usually not, unless it's affecting multiple fixtures at once, which can indicate a larger blockage.
