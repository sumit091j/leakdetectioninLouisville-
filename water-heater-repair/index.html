Water Heater Repair in Louisville, Kentucky

No hot water, a leaking tank, or a unit making unusual noise — Leak Detection In Louisville diagnoses and repairs tank and tankless water heaters for Louisville, KY homes and businesses.

Signs You Need Water Heater Repair

  • No hot water, or hot water that runs out quickly
  • Water pooling around the base of the tank
  • Rumbling, popping, or banging noises from the unit
  • Inconsistent or fluctuating water temperature
  • Discolored or rusty-looking hot water
  • Pilot light that won't stay lit (gas units)

Common Water Heater Problems

  • A failed heating element or faulty thermostat
  • Sediment buildup reducing efficiency and causing noise
  • A failing pressure relief valve
  • A corroded anode rod allowing tank corrosion
  • Gas supply or pilot light issues on gas units
  • Age-related tank failure

Gas vs. Electric Water Heaters

Gas water heaters rely on a pilot light and gas supply, while electric units depend on heating elements and thermostats — the likely failure point differs by type.

Tank vs. Tankless

Tankless water heaters heat water on demand and have different maintenance needs than tank units, including periodic descaling for sediment. See Tankless Water Heater Services.

Sediment Buildup

Sediment settling at the bottom of a tank reduces efficiency and can cause rumbling or popping noises during heating cycles.

Repair or Replace?

Age, extent of corrosion, and cost of parts all factor into whether repair or replacement makes more sense for your unit.

Water Heater Repair FAQs

Should I repair or replace my water heater?+

It depends on the unit's age, the extent of the issue, and repair cost relative to replacement. We'll walk you through the trade-offs on site.

What causes a water heater to leak?+

Leaks can come from a failed pressure relief valve, a corroded tank, or loose fittings — the source determines whether repair is possible.

Why is my water heater making noise?+

Popping or rumbling is commonly caused by sediment buildup at the bottom of the tank.

How long does a water heater typically last?+

Lifespan varies by unit type, water quality, and maintenance, so we assess condition rather than relying on age alone.
